# Probing fifteen sourced candidates: five hits, nine silences

Run on 2026-09-03 at 13:02 MST, with the vehicle parked and awake immediately
after a highway drive, adapter reading 13.9 V. The drive session was pulled to
a workstation before the recorder was stopped, so nothing recorded was at risk.

Both profiles ran under the supervised `--confirm` path against exact
enumerated identifiers. No sweep, no guessed identifier, no service other
than `22`.

## Module `CB`, battery system manager: five for five

Source: meatpiHQ/wican-fw issue #884, a 2027 Bolt on the same Ultium/BEV3
extended-addressing scheme. Unmerged, single-author, not BT1.

Every one answered.

| identifier | source's description | payload | as integer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `27BF` | charge-cycle regeneration | `00 00 21` | 33 |
| `27BB` | thermal-management energy | `00 00 64` | 100 |
| `27B5` | thermal-management distance | `00 00 15` | 21 |
| `2709` | A/C compressor temperature | `6E` | 110 |
| `2AF1` | battery module temperature | **24 bytes** | see below |

That the addressing worked was never in question — this module already answers
eight other identifiers. What is new is that five more sourced-but-unproven
candidates all returned positive responses on the first attempt.

### `0x2AF1` returns twenty-four values

```
72 73 72 72 72 72 72 72 72 72 73 73 73 73 72 72 72 72 72 72 72 72 73 72
```

Twenty-four. That is the same count as the twenty-four module-like values in
`0x2B43` (26 = 2 + 12 + 12), and it is what a per-module reading should look
like on a pack whose series count measures 96 and whose array splits into two
blocks of twelve.

The values are tightly clustered — only two distinct bytes, 114 and 115 — which
is what a thermally healthy pack should look like sitting in one place.

**On the scaling, the honest position is unproven.** The source calls this
battery module temperature. Under `(x − 40) / 2` the twenty-four land at
**37.0–37.5 °C**, against a pack temperature of **39.0 °C** measured
independently by `0x0046` in the same minute. That is close — within 1.5–2.0 °C
— and close is not proof:

* it is **one sample at one temperature**. A scaling that happens to land near
  the right answer at 39 °C says nothing about whether it holds at 5 °C or
  55 °C, and a wrong offset is invisible when you only ever look at one point.
* `0x0046` may report a maximum, a different sensor, or coolant rather than a
  module mean, so a 2 °C gap is not necessarily error at all.
* the alternative `x − 40` gives 74–75 °C, which is not obviously wrong for a
  pack that had just been driven hard, only less likely.

The bytes are worth capturing now and decoding when a temperature *range*
exists to fit against. That is the same discipline that kept `0x5401` raw until
a charging session settled it, and the same discipline that produced a refuted
hypothesis rather than a wrong column earlier the same day.

## Module `40`, body control: nine silences

Source: OBDb/Cadillac-LYRIQ PR #14, which ships real-vehicle test vectors and
is the same pull request that supplied `2414` — an identifier this vehicle does
answer. That was the entire reason to take its other register families
seriously.

| identifier | source's description | result |
|---|---|---|
| `4149` | EVSE advertised current | `NO DATA` |
| `416C` | battery group voltage 1 | `NO DATA` |
| `416D` | battery group voltage 2 | `NO DATA` |
| `416E` | battery group voltage 3 | `NO DATA` |
| `434F` | HV battery temperature | `NO DATA` |
| `4127` | battery temperature A | `NO DATA` |
| `4124` | battery temperature B | `NO DATA` |
| `40E5` | battery coolant temperature 1 | `NO DATA` |
| `40E6` | battery coolant temperature 2 | `NO DATA` |

All nine. And the distinction matters: `NO DATA` is **not** a negative
response. A `7F 22 31` would mean "this module is here, understands
ReadDataByIdentifier, and does not have that identifier" — useful, and the
result these were expected to produce at worst. `NO DATA` means **nothing
answered at all**.

So this is not evidence that the LYRIQ's identifiers are wrong. It is evidence
that **nothing responded at `14DA40F1` on this vehicle**, which is a different
and more basic finding: either the body controller does not serve `22` at that
address, or it is not reachable through the gateway the way the battery manager
and brake controller are.

The vehicle names `40` as `BCM-BodyControl` in its own service 09 module
inventory, so the module exists. It simply did not speak. Whether a different
addressing reaches it is an open question this run does not answer, and the
adapter itself was fine throughout — it returned `13.9 V` to `ATRV` before and
after.

## Second probe, 14:55 MST: reachability, answered both ways

The first probe left two questions. Nine vendor identifiers at module `40` had
returned `NO DATA`, which says nothing about the identifiers because nothing
replied — the route was what failed. And module `CD` had refused four of `CB`'s
identifiers with `7F 22 31`, which was read as "alive, with a namespace we have
not found yet."

Both were tested with **ISO 14229-1 standardised identification
DataIdentifiers** — `F187`, `F188`, `F189`, `F191`. These are not vendor
identifiers and not guesses; the standard assigns them, and an ECU implementing
service 22 is expected to answer at least some. `F190` (VIN) was deliberately
excluded: same answer, but it would pull vehicle identity into an evidence file.

### Module `40` does not answer at this address

> **This section is wrong, and the correction is below.** It is left standing
> because the reasoning in it was sound and the conclusion was still false, and
> that is worth more as a record than a quiet edit would be.

| identifier | result |
|---|---|
| `F187` vehicleManufacturerSparePartNumber | `NO DATA` |
| `F188` vehicleManufacturerECUSoftwareNumber | `NO DATA` |
| `F189` vehicleManufacturerECUSoftwareVersionNumber | `NO DATA` |
| `F191` vehicleManufacturerECUHardwareNumber | `NO DATA` |

Thirteen identifiers have now been sent to `14DA40F1` — nine vendor, four
standard — and every one drew silence. A module that answers nothing at all,
including identifiers the standard defines for exactly this purpose, is not a
module missing those identifiers. **The request is not arriving.**

That closes the question the first probe could not: testing more identifiers at
this address is wasted effort. What remains open is the *route* — a different
priority, a different session, or a path the DLC-side gateway does not bridge.

### Module `CD` refuses everything, including the standard

| identifiers | result |
|---|---|
| `F187`, `F188`, `F189`, `F191` (ISO standard) | `142AF1CD 03 7F 22 31` |
| `27C7`, `27C0`, `0046`, `5401` (proven at `CB`) | `142AF1CD 03 7F 22 31` |
| `27BF`, `27BB`, `27B5`, `2709`, `2AF1` (proven at `CB` 2026-09-03) | `142AF1CD 03 7F 22 31` |

Seventeen identifiers, one answer. `CD` **is** reachable — it replies, from its
own address, with a well-formed negative response — so it is a live service-22
speaker. But it declines `requestOutOfRange` for standard identification
DataIdentifiers as readily as for vendor ones.

This changes what `CD` looks like. The reading after the first probe was "a live
module whose namespace we have not found yet," which made it the most promising
target on the vehicle. That reading no longer fits: an ECU refusing `F187`
through `F191` is not hiding a namespace behind identifiers we have not guessed.
It is exposing **nothing** in the session it answers in.

The obvious next step — request a different diagnostic session — is service
`0x10`, which this project permanently forbids and will not add. So `CD` is not
a dead end because the identifiers are wrong; it is a dead end **from here**,
and saying so is worth more than another twenty identifiers drawing the same
three bytes.

### What the two negatives are worth

Neither module gave up data, and both experiments succeeded. Before them, the
plan pointed at `CD` as the highest-value target and at `40` as a DID problem.
After them, `CD` is closed from this access path and `40` is a routing problem
that no amount of identifier testing will solve. Two directions that would each
have absorbed a session are now marked, with the evidence for the marking.

## Correction, 15:06 MST: module 40 was never unreachable

Everything above about module `40` is wrong. Thirteen identifiers drew
`NO DATA`, and the conclusion recorded was "the request is not arriving" and
"testing more identifiers at this address is wasted effort". Both were false,
and the cause was in this project's own code rather than in the vehicle.

`_module_profile` hardcodes `ATCP14`. That is the CAN priority the battery
system manager answers at, it was established when `CB` was first opened, and
every module probed through that helper inherited it silently. Module `40` does
not answer at priority `0x14`. It answers at `0x18` — the priority the
legislated services use, and the one this project has been sending service 01
and 09 at all along.

The evidence came from a per-module support census, which asks each module what
it supports using the standard's own bitmaps rather than any vendor identifier:

```
17    DMCM-DriveMotorCtrl    svc01: 01 0D 1C 1F 21 30 31 42 A6   svc09: 02 04 06 0A
40    BCM-BodyControl        svc01: 01 42                        svc09: 04 06 0A
```

Module `40` answered. It had been answering the whole time, at a priority
nothing had asked it at. The census also proves its own receive filter isolates
rather than returning one loud responder: module `17` advertised nine service 01
PIDs where every other module advertised two.

### Every LYRIQ candidate answers at priority `0x18`

Asked again at `18DA40F1`, all nine returned positive responses:

| identifier | source's description | reply | payload |
|---|---|---|---|
| `4149` | EVSE advertised current | `62 4149 0180` | 384 |
| `416C` | battery group voltage 1 | `62 416C 0A1D` / `0A21` | 2589 / 2593 |
| `416D` | battery group voltage 2 | `62 416D 1396` | 5014 |
| `416E` | battery group voltage 3 | `62 416E 1396` | 5014 |
| `434F` | HV battery temperature | `62 434F 64` | 100 |
| `4127` | battery temperature A | `62 4127 00EE` | 238 |
| `4124` | battery temperature B | `62 4124 038E` | 910 |
| `40E5` | battery coolant temperature 1 | `62 40E5 03DE` | 990 |
| `40E6` | battery coolant temperature 2 | `62 40E6 01C1` | 449 |

The two ISO standard identifiers returned `7F 22 31` at the same address, which
is the correct shape for "I am here and do not hold that one" and confirms the
module is genuinely conversing rather than echoing.

**No scaling is claimed for any of these.** `416C` read 2589 and then 2593
across two requests a minute apart, `416D` and `416E` returned identical values,
and the vehicle was parked and unplugged, so an EVSE current of 384 raw is
being read in the one state that says least about it. These are nine new raw
payloads and nine open questions, which is exactly what the first hour of a
newly reachable module should look like.

### Module `CD` was retested and the earlier conclusion holds

The same doubt applies to `CD`, which refused seventeen identifiers at priority
`0x14`. Retested at `0x18`:

```
22F187  ->  18DAF1CD 03 7F 22 31
2227C6  ->  18DAF1CD 03 7F 22 31
222AF5  ->  18DAF1CD 03 7F 22 31
222AF1  ->  18DAF1CD 03 7F 22 31
```

`CD` refuses at both priorities, and answers from its own address at both. That
conclusion was right, and it is right for the reason originally given rather
than by luck.

### What this cost, and what it should change

The wrong conclusion was published with thirteen data points behind it and a
clean argument on top: `NO DATA` is not a negative response, therefore nothing
replied, therefore the route is the problem. Every step of that was correct. The
route *was* the problem — but the broken part of the route was a hardcoded
constant in this repository, not anything about the vehicle or its gateway.

The lesson is narrow and worth stating: **when a module is silent, vary how you
are asking before concluding anything about what you are asking for.** Thirteen
identifiers at one priority is one data point about the priority, not thirteen
about the identifiers.

## First correlations on module 40, and what they contradict

264 samples with a physically sane pack voltage (rows below 300 V were dropped;
sleep and wake edges produce transitional garbage, including a `pack_v` of
1.0 V and zeros across several module-40 fields). Correlated against quantities
this node already measures:

| field | range | distinct | vs `temp_f` | vs `soc_pct` | vs `pack_v` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `coolant_2_raw` | 440–819 | 80 | **+0.768** | −0.573 | −0.498 |
| `batt_temp_b_raw` | 0–1000 | 4 | **−0.752** | +0.512 | +0.491 |
| `batt_temp_a_raw` | 234–1048 | 4 | **+0.747** | −0.443 | −0.471 |
| `coolant_1_raw` | 945–1015 | 13 | **+0.727** | −0.268 | −0.379 |
| `group_v1_raw` | 1384–2604 | 100 | −0.691 | **+0.751** | +0.452 |
| `evse_current_raw` | 96–388 | 4 | −0.538 | +0.417 | +0.471 |
| `group_v3_raw` | 5004–5023 | 5 | +0.120 | +0.017 | −0.061 |
| `group_v2_raw` | 5004–5018 | 4 | +0.223 | −0.061 | −0.115 |
| `hv_temp_raw` | 70–70 | **1** | — | — | — |

### What this supports

The three fields the source calls **temperatures** — `0x40E5`, `0x40E6` and
`0x4127` — are the three that track the pack temperature most strongly, at
+0.73 to +0.77. That is the source's labels being corroborated by this
vehicle's own thermal field, which is the first independent evidence any of
these nine has.

### What this contradicts

The source calls `0x416C`, `0x416D` and `0x416E` **battery group voltages**.
This vehicle does not support that reading:

* `group_v2` and `group_v3` barely move at all — five distinct values across
  264 samples, spanning 5004 to 5023, correlating with nothing. Live voltages
  on a pack whose own `pack_v` swings under load do not behave that way. They
  look like limits or configuration constants.
* Their sum against pack voltage gives a ratio of **32.4**, not a round scale
  factor. Three group voltages summing to the pack should divide cleanly; this
  does not.
* `group_v1` moves plenty — 100 distinct values — but tracks **state of
  charge** (+0.75) better than pack voltage (+0.45), which is not what a group
  voltage should do.

`batt_temp_b_raw` correlates **negatively** with temperature (−0.75) while its
two siblings correlate positively. An inverted temperature is possible; so is
its being something else entirely.

### Why none of this is a scaling

`temp_f` spanned **104.0 to 109.4 °F** across the whole corpus — 5.4 °F. A
correlation computed over five degrees says something about direction and
almost nothing about slope or offset, and nothing at all about whether the
relationship stays linear at 0 °C or 50 °C. `hv_temp_raw` did not move at all
in 264 samples, so it contributes nothing yet.

What would settle these: a cold morning start, and a charge. Both put tens of
degrees through the thermal fields and take the EVSE current off its parked
value, which is the one state that says least about it. Until then these are
directions, not equations, and the columns stay raw.

## What this changes

The immediate practical result is that the battery system manager has more to
say than was being asked of it, including a twenty-four-value array that lines
up with a pack structure derived independently from voltage ratios.

The immediate practical *non*-result is that the body controller, the largest
unexplored module on this vehicle and the one carrying the most interesting
candidate list, is silent to this approach. Recording that is worth as much as
the hits: it stops the next person spending a session rediscovering it.
