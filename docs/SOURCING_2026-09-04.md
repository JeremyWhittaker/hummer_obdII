# Sourcing sweep, 2026-09-04: the motor telemetry does not exist publicly

The plan called this "the real telemetry gap": modules `17`, `1D` and `1E` are
drive motor controllers, and this project has never asked them for motor speed,
torque, inverter temperature or a power limit. The task was to find identifiers
for those and add the ones a fetchable source names exactly.

**Nothing was added.** Two independent sweeps across every public source where
such a thing would live found **no identifier, on any platform, for motor RPM,
motor torque, inverter temperature, stator temperature, motor or inverter
coolant temperature, propulsion or regen power limit, or motor phase current.**

That is not "we did not look hard enough". It is a result, and it closes an open
task: the motor telemetry gap cannot be closed by sourcing, because there is
nothing to source. Closing it needs first-hand discovery work of a kind this
project's rules do not permit — identifier sweeping is and stays forbidden.

## What was found, and why none of it was added

### Legacy Bolt is a different vehicle wearing the same badge

The richest find is real and unusable: the Bolt EV signalset carries **96
sequential per-cell voltage identifiers** (`0x4181`–`0x419F`, `0x4200`–`0x4240`),
merged, with byte-exact test vectors — `7EF05624181BFFF` → 3.74998 V. Per-cell
voltages are the single thing this project most wants and cannot get.

It does not transfer, and the reason is not subtle. Those are **11-bit** headers
(`7E7`/`7EF`) on the 2017–2023 Bolt: a different addressing scheme, a different
battery architecture, LG cell-monitoring units rather than Ultium modules. This
vehicle uses 29-bit GM extended addressing with a per-module priority byte.
Nothing about a `7EF` identifier says anything about a `142AF1CB` one.

Same for `0x4356` (pack current) and the whole Bolt charger block. They are
legacy-platform identifiers, and the project already has `0x2414` for pack
current, proven here.

### The charger identifiers have *negative* evidence on the nearest platform

`0x4368`, `0x4369`, `0x436B`, `0x436C`, `0x4373` and `0x4531` are merged and
byte-exact for the legacy Bolt, and they cover AC voltage, AC current, DC
voltage, DC current and charge mode — the charging picture this project builds
indirectly from an energy slope.

They were **tried on a real 2025 LYRIQ — BEV3, one platform generation closer to
this truck than the Bolt — and returned `NRC 0x31` requestOutOfRange in every
vehicle mode.** The LYRIQ repository's own CI probe independently records the
same DID set under `unsupported_commands_by_ecu`.

This is better than "unsourced". It is *sourced and measured negative on the
closest available platform*, which is a stronger reason not to send them than
this project usually has.

### Honda Prologue names the arrays and never says what they mean

The Prologue is BEV3 and GM-built, which makes it the most interesting
non-LYRIQ lead found. Its `command_support.yaml` names `0x2028` at modules
`DA01`/`DA16` as 168 individual cell voltages, and `0x202C` as six module
temperatures.

**No formula exists for any of them, anywhere.** The signalset that would carry
one is empty; no test case exists. And the addresses are Honda's own map, not
GM's — `DA01` and `DA16` are not among the eight modules this vehicle names for
itself. A name without a scaling and without a matching address is a lead, not a
source. It is recorded here and not in the gate.

## The one finding that changed something: a source conflict this vehicle settles

`0x33E5` is in this project's allowlist as "drive motor control module battery
voltage", `byte / 10` volts, from the merged Equinox EV signalset. Two LYRIQ
pull requests by the same author, two days apart, read that identifier
**mutually exclusively**:

| | Identifier | Claim | Same raw byte `0x84` becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PR #13, 2026-08-14 | `0x33E5` | `CADILLAC_HVBAT_V`, "HV battery pack voltage", `x 2.7`, max 450 | **356.4 V** |
| PR #14, 2026-08-16 | `0x33E5` | `LYRIQ_DMCM_BATT_V`, "DMCM battery voltage", `/ 10`, max 25.5 | **13.2 V** |

PR #14 is explicitly a correction, and says so: *"`2233E5` is NOT pack
voltage... on this vehicle it is the 12 V rail, matching the naming already used
by OBDb/Chevrolet-Equinox-EV."* Both are still unmerged, so a reader arriving at
that repository today finds two open pull requests disagreeing by a factor of 27
and no merged answer.

**This vehicle settles it, and had already settled it.** Modules `17`, `1D` and
`1E` each answer `0x33E5` independently with 13.2 / 13.1 / 13.1 V, in the same
minute the pack read 382.65 V from `0x2885`. A pack-voltage reading would be
that second number. Three modules agreeing on the 12 V figure is a measurement
the source dispute does not have.

Worth being clear about what that does and does not do. It confirms **this
project is on the correct side of an unresolved public disagreement**, which is
worth writing down. It does not confirm the `/10` divisor to better than the
6 % this project already has open against the adapter's own `ATRV` reading of
the same rail. `0x33E5` stays at confidence level 3.

> **Correction, later on 2026-09-04.** The sentence above, and the
> multiplicative reading of the three-way difference that stood in
> `confidence.py` alongside it, are superseded. The 6 % is not an open
> disagreement: the three readings are one rail separated by *stable additive
> offsets*, and the evidence originally cited for a multiplicative difference
> — "ratios hold to 0.53 % while offsets wander by 23 %" — compares the
> relative spread of a quantity with a 0.29 V mean against one with a 1.02
> mean, and is in any case exactly what a fixed offset produces, since
> y = x + c implies y/x = 1 + c/x. Refitting both models by least squares and
> comparing residuals in volts, additive wins every pair across the full
> corpus. `0x33E5` does stay at level 3, for the reason given — no reference
> meter — but not for the reason argued here. See the 12 V section of
> `TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md` for the numbers.

## A smaller corroboration: `0x2AF1` really is an array here

A raw ELM327 debug log attached to `meatpiHQ/wican-fw` issue #497, captured from
a **GMC Sierra EV** — BT1, the same platform family — shows `0x2AF1` at module
`CB` answering with a **27-byte multi-frame response**, array-shaped.

This project reads 24 values from that identifier and stores them raw. The Bolt
issue that supplied the identifier gives a single-byte formula
(`[B5:B5] * 1.8 - 112`), which cannot be right for a payload of that length.
Someone else's BT1 vehicle answering with an array is independent support for
treating it as one — and for having declined to apply the source's scaling.

The log carries no decode table, so no name or formula can be attributed to it.
It moves nothing above level 1. It is recorded because a second BT1 vehicle
behaving the same way is the kind of evidence that is easy to lose and hard to
get again.

## What this means for the plan

The plan's G4 assumed sourcing work would produce identifiers to probe. It
produced none, and the honest conclusion is that the drive motor controllers'
internals are not publicly documented for any GM Ultium vehicle. What remains
reachable at those modules is what is already there: `0x2885` pack voltage,
`0x2414` pack current, `0x33E5` module supply voltage, and `0x2429` — which is
now, finally, sendable.

`0x2429` was sent that same day, for the first time since it was allowlisted,
and **answered**: `0x5806` = 22534. Under the source's `/64` that is 352.09 V —
3.6676 V across 96 series cells, the textbook nominal for an NMC cell, which was
convincing enough to publish and turned out to be a coincidence. Across 405
samples the field moves with load (r = +0.83 against pack current) and is stored
raw; see [the catalog](TELEMETRY_CATALOG.md). The source's label is the second
one this vehicle has contradicted.

So the sweep's one concrete gain is not a new identifier at all, and not even a
decoded one. It is having noticed that an old identifier had never been used —
and then having enough samples to find out that the obvious reading of it was
wrong.

The gap is real and it stays open. The correct next move is not a wider search;
it is to stop expecting one to work.
